# ADR 0020 — The T.O.M. reconciliation is deferred to the end; a provisional URL lock holds the line until then

- **Status:** Accepted, 2026-08-21
- **Deciders:** S. Le Boulanger
- **Supersedes:** ADR 0019 (its sequencing only — its analysis and its exposure figures stand unchanged and are not restated here)
- **Related:** ADR 0003 (URLs are frozen on three axes), ADR 0015 (VG Wort operations), ADR 0002 (the gate battery)
- **Unblocks:** all of Phase 1, and P3.2 / P3.3 / P3.4a — the flat → leaf-bundle conversions

## Context

ADR 0019 made the T.O.M. export the first action in the programme and blocked every URL-affecting step behind it. That ordering rested on a conflation which, on re-examination, does not hold: **the export answers two different questions, and only one of them gates the work.**

**Question A — neutrality.** *Does this change move a URL?* This is a property of the repository and nothing else. Build the site before the change, build it after, diff the two `sitemap.xml` files. A change that moves no URL cannot orphan a mark, whatever T.O.M. holds. No external fact is required to answer it.

**Question B — correctness.** *Does each mark's registered URL match the URL it renders on today?* Only the T.O.M. account can answer this, and ADR 0019 documents the measured evidence that up to 592 of the 821 marks may already be wrong — evidence that stands.

ADR 0019 blocked Question-A work on a Question-B answer. Almost the entire programme is Question A. The whole of Phase 1 — the `kit` module, design tokens, the font system, the materials pipeline, `audiogen`, the curriculum resolver — moves layouts, styles, scripts and generated artefacts, and moves no URL at all. Even the flat → leaf-bundle conversions are Question A: ADR 0003 chose leaf bundles *precisely because* they are URL-neutral under all three axes, and `fle`'s front-matter `slug:` overrides are what preserve its URLs through the conversion. Whether that neutrality actually holds in a given commit is provable from the repository, by diff, with no reference to any registration.

So the export gates one thing: **repairing divergence that already exists.** It gates nothing about preventing new divergence, and nothing about the platform work.

## Decision

**The T.O.M. reconciliation moves to the final phase of the programme. Until it runs, the URL space is frozen against a provisional, repository-derived lock, and gate A3 enforces neutrality rather than correctness.**

**1. The provisional lock.** `vgwort/url-lock-provisional.csv` is generated from the built `sitemap.xml` crossed with `vgwort/marks.yaml`, with schema `url,code,first_seen,content_sha` — note the **absence** of a `registered_url` column, which is the whole difference from the real lock and the reason for the filename. It is regenerated by `bin/kit vgwort lock --provisional` and carries a header comment stating in one line that it records where marks *render*, not where they *earn*, and that it is not evidence of reconciliation.

**2. Gate A3 runs in neutrality mode.** It blocks any pull request whose built sitemap differs from the locked set, unless the diff is accompanied by an explicit `url-change:` entry naming each moved URL and its mark. This is strictly stronger than what exists today, which is nothing — `efl/.github/workflows/hugo.yml:128` carries the org's only URL-parity check and it is suppressed with `continue-on-error`. From the day the provisional lock lands, **no new divergence can be introduced**, by this programme or by anyone.

**3. What the deferral does not buy back.** The provisional lock cannot detect divergence that predates it. If the 592 marks are registered against Quarto `.html` URLs, they continue to earn nothing for the duration of the programme, and that loss is not recoverable retroactively — VG Wort counts accesses in the period they occur. This is the accepted cost, recorded here rather than discovered later.

**4. `fle`'s missing dates ride along.** P3.3 lands all 349 `fle` front-matter marks in `vgwort/marks.yaml` with `registered_at: unknown-pre-programme`, per ADR 0015. Gate C1 therefore runs **warn on `fle` for the whole programme** instead of the single release ADR 0015 anticipated, and becomes blocking only after the export backfills the dates. `efl` and `daf` are unaffected — their dates are in-repo.

**5. The reconciliation, when it runs**, follows ADR 0019 step for step: export, diff into `vgwort/tom-diff.csv`, dispose of every `alias-only` row by T.O.M.-side URL edit where VG Wort permits one and by the ADR 0015 re-key protocol where it does not, and record the finding in `.github/docs/vgwort-operations.md`. The provisional lock is then replaced by `vgwort/url-lock.csv` with its `registered_url` column populated, and A3 switches from neutrality to correctness. Nothing about that procedure changes; only when it happens.

**6. It is not optional and it is not open-ended.** The reconciliation is a release gate on the programme's completion: no course may declare `conformance_status: met` and no repository may cut its first CalVer tag under the completed programme while `vgwort/url-lock-provisional.csv` still exists in it. The provisional file is the reminder, and its filename is chosen so that it cannot be mistaken for the finished artefact in a directory listing.

## Consequences

- Phase 1 starts immediately. Nothing in it touches a URL, and the provisional lock proves that per commit rather than asserting it.
- The content-model migrations proceed on the strength of a before/after sitemap diff, which is the evidence that actually establishes what they claim.
- The org gains a working URL gate months earlier than ADR 0019 allowed, because a repository-derived baseline needs no portal, no export and no waiting.
- The 592-mark exposure, if real, persists for the programme's duration. This is the price of the deferral and it is stated as such.
- One more artefact exists that must be deleted before the programme can be called finished, and it is named so that it is noticed.
- `fle` carries a warn-level C1 for longer than any other repository. That is visible in every one of its builds, which is the intended reminder.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep ADR 0019's ordering.** Rejected on the finding above: it blocks Question-A work on a Question-B answer, which stops the entire platform programme on an external portal task with unbounded latency. The analysis in 0019 was right; the dependency it drew from that analysis was wider than the facts support.

**Defer T.O.M. and ship no URL gate at all until it lands.** Rejected. It leaves the URL space unprotected for the duration of exactly the work most likely to move a URL, and it wastes the fact that neutrality is cheaply provable without the export. Deferring the repair is a decision; declining the prevention would be an oversight.

**Generate the real `url-lock.csv` from the sitemap and treat it as authoritative.** Rejected, and this is the rejection ADR 0015 and ADR 0019 already recorded. A lock with a `registered_url` column filled in from the sitemap asserts a fact nobody checked, and A3 would then defend it permanently. The provisional file omits the column rather than guessing at it, which is why it is a different file with a different name and not a stage of the same one.

**Hand-sample twenty codes now instead of a full export.** Not rejected — held. Twenty codes answer the yes/no question at a fraction of the effort, and if the answer is "already correct" the whole exposure closes for two hours of work. It remains available at any point and should be taken the first time the portal is open for any other reason. It is not scheduled, because scheduling it is what this ADR defers.
