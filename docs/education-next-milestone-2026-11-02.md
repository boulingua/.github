## Ordered outcomes and completion criteria

1. **Make assessments executable and preserve the intended skill.** Audit stimulus availability, learner/teacher separation and task-to-key fidelity before lengthening a paper. The EFL inventory finds 150 papers with listening instructions: 145 print the listening transcript to learners (142 listening-only tasks and three listen/read alternatives), while five other papers lack a complete standalone stimulus. Repair these source-specific defects in bounded cohorts. Some advanced papers reuse the listening text for reading; supply phased paper instructions rather than hiding text needed by another task. Completion: every scored response is supported by supplied material, the learner copy cannot expose listening answers during the listening phase, and a teacher can deliver the task from the standalone bundle. Native and pedagogical review remains separately recorded.
2. **Develop the 302 short teaching units and 227 short assessment pages.** Add missing models, meaningful guided practice, information-gap or source-based tasks, transfer and reflection evidence linked to each unit's objectives. Preserve useful existing work, URLs and original source archives. First pilot: EFL `track-e/kl05/units/unit01-hello-world` and its sibling assessment. Its existing 412 learner words do not supply enough modelled, supported and independently transferable work. The staged derivative adds contrasts, task-specific language support, paired information exchange, reporting, revision and objective-linked reflection; it also supplies a teacher-only listening script and explicit item criteria. Completion: independent source/task/key review, canonical source and rendered counts, successful learner/teacher export with no missing content, and realistic learner and teacher trials. Count compliance is necessary under the existing contract, not proof of educational quality.
3. **Resolve assessment metadata and classroom feasibility.** Infer point maxima only from actual additive rubrics; document whether a task is a rehearsal, points-only paper or uses a reviewed grade conversion. Draft duration estimates from the actual sequence, then record timed classroom trials and adaptations. Completion: all 188 missing durations are resolved and the 12 explicitly declared rehearsals have an accepted scope disposition under the assessment contract, without fabricated observations. A native-language checker and teacher must judge level, ambiguity, workload and marking consistency.
4. **Review coverage and Programme I progression.** All task mappings remain proposed. The 1,199-entry framework is populated, including 29 source-restored entries pending editorial approval; it does not make the course maps complete. Review each mapping against the actual task, collect evidence for gaps and author the remaining planned units. The eleven Programme I courses each have one substantive A1 unit/assessment and pronunciation draft, not a complete A1 course or a completed M2 milestone. Completion: each course's accepted milestone requirements and reviewed task coverage are met. The four deferred courses retain their own documented re-entry conditions.
5. **Regenerate and revalidate only accepted source revisions.** After each source cohort is reviewed, refresh exact source hashes, materials, audio candidate inputs, scoring and structural gates together. Preserve superseded inputs and receipts. Close technical findings with actual exports and rendered checks; close human criteria with dated reviewers and artifact hashes. Release the accepted scope explicitly, without relabelling unfinished full courses as complete.

Engineering and authorship can supply coherent drafts, exact sources, executable assessments, safe exports and truthful review packets now. Native-language judgments, classroom timing, suitability of grades and final adoption are human decisions; pending status must remain visible. Synthetic voices can be created on this machine, but synthesis is not a dated pronunciation, naturalness or appropriateness verdict.
